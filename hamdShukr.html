بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ

ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ

Sūrah Al‑Fātiḥah · 1:2

The Qur'an opens with praise. Before law, before story, before warning — al‑ḥamd. This is a living companion for the two words that reorder a heart: ḥamd (praise that is owed) and shukr (thanks that is returned) — to Allah, and then to the parents and people He placed in your path.

Part One · ٱلْحَمْد

Ḥamd is praise that would be true even if no one gave it.

Shukr responds to a favour received. Ḥamd is wider: it is praise for who Allah is — His perfection, His names, His wisdom — whether or not the gift has yet arrived. Notice how many sūrahs Allah begins by praising Himself, teaching us the words to use.

Part Two · ٱلشُّكْر

Gratitude is not a mood. It is a transaction Allah guarantees.

The Qur'an frames shukr as recognition (heart), declaration (tongue), and obedience (limbs) — and attaches to it one of the most emphatic promises in the entire Book.

Anatomy of thanks

Shukr has three organs. Miss one and it limps.

A classical framework (developed by scholars such as Ibn al‑Qayyim) drawn from how the Qur'an itself describes grateful servants.

Part Three · شُكْرُ ٱلْوَالِدَيْنِ وَٱلنَّاس

Allah paired thanks to Himself with thanks to your parents — in one command.

In Sūrah Luqmān the imperative arrives undivided: أَنِ ٱشْكُرْ لِى وَلِوَٰلِدَيْكَ. Vertical gratitude and horizontal gratitude are not two projects. Ingratitude to the people who carried you is a rehearsal for ingratitude to the One who made them carry you.

The du'ā' Allah taught for parents

وَقُل رَّبِّ ٱرْحَمْهُمَا كَمَا رَبَّيَانِى صَغِيرًا

wa qul rabbi rḥamhumā kamā rabbayānī ṣaghīrā

“And say, ‘My Lord, be merciful to them as they raised me when I was small.’”

Al‑Isrā' · 17:24

Read the exchange: they gave you tarbiyah you could never repay, so Allah redirects the debt — you ask Ar‑Rabb, the ultimate Nurturer, to repay them on your behalf, in a currency you don't possess: His mercy.
