Chapter 3 · Verse 9

Our Lord, You will surely gather all people for a Day with no doubt in it. Allah never breaks His appointment.

Inside the verse

01

Who's saying this?

Verse 7 splits readers in two. Some have a tilt in their hearts. They chase the unclear verses to stir up confusion.

Others are rooted in knowledge. They say, “We believe in all of it. It's all from our Lord.” This prayer is theirs.

02

Why bring up that Day here?

The tilted hearts want the final meaning of everything, right now. The rooted ones can wait.

They know the full picture comes on one Day. Verse 7:53 says it plainly: “On the Day its final meaning arrives…” Being sure of that Day makes you patient with what you don't know yet.

03

It asks for nothing

Verse 8 asks, “Don't let our hearts slip.” Verse 9 asks for nothing. It just says what they're sure of.

That's how a heart stays steady. You keep reminding it where everything ends.

04

A noun, not a verb

It doesn't say “You will gather.” It says “You are the Gatherer.”

A verb is something that will happen. A noun is who He is. It's already settled.

سَتَجْمَعُYou will gather
جَامِعُThe Gatherer
05

The same three words, twice

Chapter 2 opens with: “That Book. No doubt in it.” This verse says: “A Day. No doubt in it.”

The Book with no doubt tells you about the Day with no doubt. Trust the first, and you're ready for the second.

ذَٰلِكَ ٱلْكِتَٰبُ لَا رَيْبَ فِيهِ2:2 · The Book

لِيَوْمٍ لَّا رَيْبَ فِيهِ3:9 · The Day

06

Not just “unsure”

رَيْب isn't plain doubt. It's the restless kind. The nagging feeling that won't let you settle.

The verse wipes it out completely. About this Day, your heart can be still.

07

From “You” to “Allah”

They start by talking to Him: “Surely You…” Then they switch: “Surely Allah…”

Now they're talking about Him, out loud, to anyone listening and to themselves. The prayer becomes a declaration. Some read that last line as Allah Himself confirming their words.

إِنَّكَSurely You
إِنَّ ٱللَّهَSurely Allah
08

An appointment, not just a promise

وَعْد is a promise. مِيعَاد is a promise with a set time and place. The date is already fixed.

And يُخْلِفُ comes from خَلْف, “behind.” Allah never leaves a promise behind.

ٱلْمِيعَادَ

Everything scattered gets gathered.
Every appointment gets kept.

Live like it's on the calendar. It is.
