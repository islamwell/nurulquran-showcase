The Standing Miracle

Every prophet was
given a sign.

Mūsā had a staff. ʿĪsā healed the blind. But the final Prophet ﷺ was given something that would not fade with the generation that saw it — a text. Its miracle, called iʿjāz, is that it cannot be matched: not in structure, not in cadence, not in the seamless coherence of 6,236 verses revealed piecemeal across twenty‑three years.

The Qurʾān makes that claim testable. It invites its critics — the finest poets of a civilisation built on poetry — to produce a single comparable chapter. The challenge has never been closed.

Closing

A miracle, in the Qurʾānic vocabulary, is called an āyah — a sign. Not a spectacle to be consumed, but an arrow pointing beyond itself. The splitting moon, the parted sea, the cooled fire: each was temporary. The invitation they carried was not.
